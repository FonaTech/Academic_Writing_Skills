# Journal policies on AI-generated figures and AI use

Policies change. Re-check the target journal's current author guidelines before submission and record the date you checked. The Wiley and Springer Nature summaries were read from the publishers' pages on 2026-10-01. The Science page could not be retrieved that day, so its entry reflects the earlier published policy and must be checked.

## Wiley (including Advanced Materials, Advanced Materials Technologies, Advanced Functional Materials)
Source: Wiley AI guidelines for authors, https://www.wiley.com/en-us/publish/article/ai-guidelines/

- AI-edited photographs are not permitted. Factual and evidential images (results, experimental data, micrographs, diagnostic images) must not be generated, modified or enhanced by AI.
- Conceptual illustrations, workflow diagrams, frameworks and flowcharts drawn or edited with AI are permitted with disclosure **in the figure caption**; original images may be requested.
- Data visualisations made with AI require disclosure and accuracy checks; dedicated plotting software is preferred to general-purpose models.
- Disclosure locations: Acknowledgments for drafting, editing, translation or formatting help; Methods for AI in methodology, data collection, analysis or literature review; figure captions for any AI-generated or AI-edited visual. Name the tool and version, the date, its role, the affected sections and how the authors checked the output.
- Typically no disclosure needed: simple grammar correction, formatting, word-choice suggestions, citation formatting, reference management, and **visual drafts that do not appear in the final paper or are rebuilt in standard software**.
- References: check that every citation corresponds to a real publication and that the cited source makes the argument attributed to it; fabricated citations are an integrity concern.
- AI cannot be an author; authors are fully responsible for all content.

## Springer Nature (Nature Portfolio)
Source: https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/ai (risk-assessment framework)

- Green (permitted; disclosure encouraged): language polishing, structuring sections, translation, stress-testing questions.
- Amber (permitted with human oversight, verification and disclosure): drafting explanatory summaries, comparing results with the literature, extensive copy editing or writing support, suggesting analyses.
- Red (not permitted): fabricating data, citations or results; presenting AI-generated hypotheses or conclusions as human-derived; assigning authorship to AI; creating photorealistic images (deepfakes); uploading manuscripts to unsecured AI tools.
- The framework page read on 2026-10-01 names only photorealistic images (deepfakes) as prohibited, but a parallel Springer Nature page still carries the older statement that legal issues around AI-generated images remain unresolved. Until the editor confirms otherwise, assume the older rule: no generative-AI images in published figures. Earlier Nature Portfolio policy did not accept generative-AI images in published figures except in narrow cases; the image-integrity page gave no AI-specific rule that day. Confirm with the editor before submitting any AI-drawn panel.

## Science journals (AAAS)
Check the current editorial policies; AAAS has required explicit permission from the editors for AI-generated images and disclosure of AI use in the cover letter and acknowledgments. Assume AI-drawn figures are not accepted without permission.

## Practical rules for this skill set
1. Treat Nano Banana Pro renderings as **drafts** unless the target journal explicitly allows AI-drawn conceptual illustrations. Redraw the final schematic in standard software (PowerPoint, Illustrator, Inkscape, BioRender with its licence) using the draft as a brief; redrawn drafts typically need no disclosure under Wiley's rules, but record the workflow.
2. If an AI-drawn conceptual panel stays in the final figure, disclose it in the caption: "Panel **a** drafted with Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image, accessed <month year>) and edited by the authors."
3. Never use an image model on data panels, micrographs, photographs or any evidential image.
4. Reuse of published panels: CC BY content with "Adapted from ref. X under a CC BY 4.0 licence"; otherwise request permission (RightsLink) and record it. Keep `sources.csv` in each figure folder: DOI, figure and panel, licence, route (CC BY / RightsLink / redrawn), credit line.
5. Disclose AI assistance with drafting or editing in Acknowledgments (Wiley) or as the journal requires; state that all AI-assisted text was reviewed and revised by the authors.
