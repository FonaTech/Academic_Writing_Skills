# Captions and tables

This guide covers figure captions, panel clauses, call-outs, abbreviation definitions, table notes and table design for any field. Examples use placeholders (⟨object⟩, ⟨quantity⟩, ⟨condition⟩, ⟨value⟩).

## 1 Figure policy

Data panels always come from code, drawn from a JSON file in which every value has a source, page and evidence level. No data panel is ever regenerated by an image model. By default (route D), image-model output is a draft that the author redraws in standard software. An AI-drawn panel stays in a final figure only when the journal allows AI illustrations, and the caption then discloses it.

## 2 Rules

### Captions

1. **Build each caption from a label, a title, panel clauses in panel order, shared conditions, abbreviations and credits.** Never open a caption with a panel label such as "(a)".
2. **Make each caption self-contained, at the length typical of its genre.** A reader who sees only the figure and its caption should be able to follow it. Aim for about 60–150 words for a review figure and 90–170 for an article figure.
3. **Title the figure with a noun phrase of 5–12 words that names the object and the measurement.** Leave evaluative adjectives out. A claim title suits a figure whose panels all support one finding.
4. **Put each condition inside the panel clause it conditions.** Instrument settings, doses, stimuli, temperatures, sample sizes or model parameters go with their panel. Gather shared conditions in one closing sentence.
5. **Give n, the error-bar definition and the averaging wherever data are aggregated.**
6. **Label the evidence level and the accounting scope in the title or the panel clause.** Evidence levels are measured, simulated from measured inputs, projected, extrapolated and abstract-only. The scope says what level a value refers to and what it includes. A model panel says "model".
7. **Expand every abbreviation at the end of the caption that uses it.** Table notes do the same, even when the text has defined it: "⟨ABBR1⟩, ⟨expansion⟩; ⟨ABBR2⟩, ⟨expansion⟩."
8. **In a review, caption each reused panel in your own words and credit its source.** The credit line follows the journal's house format; original schematics need none. Remove the source's voice ("our", "proposed").
9. **State the population, source, cut-off date and normalisation of a figure compiled from the literature.** Define any composite axis in the caption.
10. **Use a colon, not "namely", to introduce a list in a caption.** "Three routes: ⟨A⟩, ⟨B⟩ and ⟨C⟩." In Chinese, "即" is acceptable.

### Call-outs

11. **Use the house call-out syntax of the target journal.** Nature-family journals use "Fig. 1a", "Fig. 1a,b", "Supplementary Fig. 3" and "Extended Data Fig. 2", with "Figure 1" at the start of a sentence. The Science family uses "Fig. 1A" and "fig. S3". Wiley, ACS, Elsevier and IEEE each have their own forms; follow the author guide.
12. **Write each call-out as the result, its condition and n, then the parenthetical call-out.** Keep "as shown in" rare. Number figures by first mention, so Fig. 3 never comes before Fig. 2. Say what the figure shows, with no sentence about how to read it.

### Tables

13. **Set tables in three-line style.** Heavy top rule, a rule under the header and a heavy bottom rule, with no vertical rules. A one-sentence title above, notes below, units in the column headers. References go in a dedicated column.
14. **Give a comparative review at least one metric benchmark table in the main text.** A free-text table of pros and cons does not count.
15. **Add a Year column generated from reference metadata.** Place it after the reference column. The builder fills it from the reference list, so it never drifts from the citations; never type years by hand. A cell that cites several works shows the range ("2019–2024"); separator rows for groups stay blank in the Year column. A no-citation edition keeps the years and the group labels; group labels never live in the Ref cell.
16. **Order benchmark columns as identity, year, structure or design, test conditions, metrics with units, evidence level and scale.** Put each condition beside the metric it conditions and each property in its own column.
17. **Keep one quantity and one unit per column, and numbers only in numeric cells.** Use one missing-data code ("n.s." for not stated, or "n.r." for not reported), define it in the note, and translate it with a fixed term in the second language.
18. **Let footnotes or the title carry the normalisation, the evidence level and the inclusion criterion.** Add an Exp./Sim./Proj. column when a table mixes measured and simulated results, and a scope column for aggregate values.
19. **Cite each table once in the text, where the argument needs it, and do not restate its rows.** Tables do not count toward body-word budgets unless the venue says so.
20. **Check every number that appears twice, in caption, text, table or abstract.** Copy the value from one source record instead of retyping it.

## 3 Templates

Journals set the figure label and the panel letters in bold, which the plain-text templates below do not show.

### 3.1 Article caption (Nature-family house style)

```
Fig. N | [Object + measurement, 5-12-word noun phrase].
a, [Quantity plotted] of [object] under [conditions]. b, ... (one clause per panel)
[Shared conditions.] [Statistics: n, error-bar definition.] [Evidence level if mixed.] [ABBR, expansion.]

Shape: Fig. 3 | ⟨Response⟩ of ⟨object⟩ to ⟨stimulus⟩. a, ⟨Quantity⟩ under ⟨N⟩ repeated ⟨stimuli⟩
(⟨settings⟩); line, median of n = ⟨N⟩ ⟨units⟩; band, interquartile range. b, ⟨Quantity⟩ after
⟨duration⟩ at ⟨condition⟩; dashed lines, extrapolation. c, Simulated ⟨task metric⟩ using the values
in a. Measured at ⟨shared condition⟩. ⟨ABBR⟩, ⟨expansion⟩.
```

### 3.2 Review caption for reprinted panels

```
Figure N. [Topic + the comparison the figure makes].
a) [Work or route]: [what the panel shows + the one number or condition that matters]. b) ...
[Credit line per source, in the journal's format.]

Shape: Figure 5. Two routes to ⟨property⟩ in ⟨subject⟩. a) ⟨Route A⟩ under identical ⟨stimuli⟩.
b) ⟨Route B⟩, which costs ⟨penalty⟩. a) Reproduced with permission.[45] Copyright ⟨year⟩, ⟨publisher⟩.
b) Adapted from ref. 52 under a CC BY 4.0 licence.
```

### 3.3 Benchmark-map caption (literature data)

```
Fig. N | [Metric Y] versus [metric X] of [population, years].
Markers, [grouping]; numbers, references. Values at [level], normalised to [basis].
Sources: [venues or search, cut-off date]. Open markers, simulated or extrapolated. [Axis definitions.]
```

### 3.4 Review figure with a claim title and a model panel

```
⟨Factor⟩ sets the ⟨cost⟩ of ⟨process⟩. a, ⟨Process⟩ from ⟨input⟩ through ⟨stages⟩ to ⟨output⟩, with
the ⟨cost⟩ term of each stage. b, Three strategies: ⟨A⟩, ⟨B⟩ and ⟨C⟩. c, ⟨Requirement⟩ against
⟨scale variable⟩ (model). d, Reported shares of ⟨cost⟩ by stage; dots are single values and bars span
reported ranges; scopes differ and are not averaged. ⟨ABBR⟩, ⟨expansion⟩.
```

### 3.5 Table skeletons (three-line)

```
Comparison table   Ref. | Year (generated) | ⟨Subject or route⟩ | ⟨Design or structure⟩ | Scale or sample (n)
                   | Conditions | ⟨Metric 1⟩ (unit) | ⟨Metric 2⟩ (unit) | Evidence (Exp./Sim./Proj.) | Scope
Application map    Application | Property used | Best reported demonstration | Comparator | Next decisive result
Design rules       Effect | Origin | Governing relation | Symptom | Mitigation | What to report
Footnotes          a, ⟨what the metric includes and excludes⟩. b, normalised to ⟨basis⟩. n.s., not stated in the source.
```

With few works, transpose the table so that the works become columns.

### 3.6 Call-out sentence

```
[Result with number] under [condition] ([n, scope]) (Fig. Nx).
Shape: Across ⟨N⟩ ⟨units⟩ the ⟨quantity⟩ varied by ⟨value⟩ (s.d./mean) under ⟨condition⟩ (Fig. 3a).
```

## 4 Anti-patterns and fixes

| Pattern | Before (constructed) | After |
|---|---|---|
| Title-only caption | "Fig. 2. ⟨Topic⟩." | title + panel clauses + conditions + n + abbreviations |
| Evaluative title | "Excellent ⟨property⟩ of ⟨object⟩" | "⟨Property⟩ of ⟨object⟩ under ⟨condition⟩" |
| Missing statistics | "Error bars are shown." | "Error bars, s.d. over n = ⟨N⟩ ⟨units⟩." |
| Mixed missing-data codes | "NR", "not given", "–" in one table | one code ("n.s."), defined in the note |
| Hand-typed years | years typed into cells, drifting from the references | a generated Year column |
| "namely" list | "three routes, namely A, B and C" | "three routes: A, B and C" |
| Table restated in text | a paragraph walking through every row | one citation of the table where the argument needs it |

## 5 Venue differences

Nature-family journals use "Fig. N |" with a bold title, short panel clauses and statistics in every caption. Science-family journals use "Fig. N." with capital panel letters. Wiley and ACS reviews often use "Figure N." with credit lines after each panel. IEEE uses "Fig. N." and short captions. Check the author guide for caption length limits and table formatting.

## 6 Checks

- The title is a noun phrase that names the object and the measurement, with no evaluative adjective.
- Panel clauses follow the panel letters, and each carries its own conditions.
- Every aggregated panel gives n, the error-bar definition and the averaging.
- The evidence level and the accounting scope are stated wherever measured, simulated and projected values mix.
- Every abbreviation in a caption or table is expanded at the end of the caption or in the table note.
- Every reused panel has a credit line and a sources.csv row, and an AI-drawn panel that stays is disclosed.
- A figure or table compiled from the literature states its population, source, cut-off date, normalisation and inclusion criterion.
- Tables are three-line, with units in the headers, one quantity per column, a generated Year column and a defined missing-data code.
- Every number shared by caption, table and text is identical.
