# Referee simulation

Simulated reports help find weaknesses before submission. They are written from the manuscript, the evidence tables and the reference reviews; they never claim to predict a decision.

## Panel
Choose two or three referees whose expertise differs:
- a **subject expert** on the paper's core material, mechanism or method (how the results were obtained, statistics, reproducibility);
- an **integration or scale expert** who asks how the result carries to the next level (accounting scope, what is included, benchmarks against the established approach);
- an **applications or users** expert (relevance of the task or population, datasets, fair baselines, what the results enable).
Adapt the three roles to the field: e.g. clinician, epidemiologist and statistician; theorist, experimentalist and modeller; field ecologist, remote-sensing expert and policy analyst.
For articles add a **methods** referee (controls, error bars, sample sizes, data availability).

## Report format (per referee)
1. Summary of the manuscript in three sentences, in the referee's own words.
2. Overall assessment: significance, novelty relative to cited reviews, soundness, clarity.
3. Major comments, numbered, each with the location, the concern, why it matters and what would resolve it.
4. Minor comments, numbered, with locations.
5. Recommendation with a one-sentence justification.

## Rules
- Each comment must cite a location and, where it concerns facts, the evidence.
- Ask for what a real referee could reasonably demand: missing comparators, scope statements, statistics, clarifications, fairer benchmarks.
- Avoid vague praise and vague criticism ("the writing could be improved"); name the paragraph and the problem.
- Referees criticise the manuscript, not the cited authors; a gap in the cited literature is a request for a field-level statement, not for per-paper faults.
- End with a merged list of actions ranked by effort and impact.
