# Fair comparisons across fields

Check that compared quantities measure the same defined outcome in compatible objects, populations, tasks and conditions. Preserve denominators, units, analysis/model choices, uncertainty and accounting scope. If normalization is justified, record the formula, inputs and assumptions; otherwise show the differences instead of ranking incomparable values.

Examples: absolute versus relative clinical risk; raw versus standardized educational scores; nominal versus real economic outcomes; concentration versus total amount in chemistry; per-run versus aggregated computing performance; local trend versus global/scenario projections; mathematical bounds versus empirical averages. These examples identify potential mismatches, not a universal normalization method.

Separate observed, simulated, derived and projected results; record primary/secondary source roles and access limits independently. A secondary summary cannot supply unreported primary conditions. An equal numerical value does not establish equivalence.
