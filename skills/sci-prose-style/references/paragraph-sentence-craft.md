# Paragraphs and sentences across fields

Start from the paragraph's question. Establish the topic, present the relevant evidence/reasoning, explain what follows and preserve the limits. A proof, qualitative interpretation and empirical comparison need different internal structures; no fixed sequence applies to all paragraphs.

Default precise style targets one principal claim per sentence and a 16–20-word English median, with sentences usually no longer than about 30 words. Retain equations, conditions and qualifiers when scientifically necessary. Use active subjects where appropriate and avoid repetitive staccato prose.

Synthetic teaching example, with no claim of an observed study:

Before: “It is important to note that the results are remarkable and pave the way for future applications.”

After: “The paired comparison estimates the difference between the specified conditions while retaining uncertainty from the repeated observations.”

The revision names what the comparison does without inventing a numerical result. In a real manuscript, tie it to the actual method and uncertainty calculation. Do not call a comparison paired unless the design establishes the pairing.
