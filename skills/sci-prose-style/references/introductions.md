# Introductions

Define the problem and why it matters to the intended reader. Summarize what is established, identify the unresolved question with supporting literature and explain what the manuscript contributes. Avoid claiming that no prior work exists without a documented search.

Original research introduces the question, relevant knowledge and the actual design/contribution. A review explains selection scope and the synthesis question; systematic/scoping work states its applicable reporting approach. Theory states the problem, assumptions and relation to existing results. Qualitative work motivates the context and interpretive approach without forcing a quantitative hypothesis.

Select the appropriate public reporting/source reference through the cross-domain case bank. Default precise prose uses concrete topic sentences, evidence-calibrated claims and restrained previews; it does not impose a fixed paragraph count or a single disciplinary architecture.
