# Titles and abstracts

Follow the actual venue's length and structure. Title the scientific object/question and contribution precisely; avoid unjustified priority, praise and strings of modifiers. A title does not have a universal word ceiling.

An empirical abstract states the question, relevant design, supported result and calibrated interpretation. A synthesis states scope, method where applicable, main synthesis and limits. Theory states the problem, result, assumptions and relevance. A proposal describes planned work and uncertainty, never completed results that do not exist.

Draft the abstract after the main argument stabilizes. Check every quantity/citation/causal claim against the body. Apply default precise style without deleting scientifically necessary qualifiers. Do not add a result because an abstract template expects one.
