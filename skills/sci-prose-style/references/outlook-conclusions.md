# Discussion and conclusions

Explain what the supported finding changes, how it compares with prior evidence, which alternatives remain and where the interpretation stops. Distinguish limitations of measurement, design, model, selection and generalization. A conclusion should follow the argument rather than repeat a generic promise.

Research priorities identify a specific question and the observation, design or proof needed to answer it. Quantified targets require a source or explicit rationale; do not invent them. Proposals state planned work as planned. Qualitative insights do not automatically estimate population prevalence; predictive success does not by itself establish causality.

Default precise writing names consequences directly, retains uncertainty and avoids stock endings. Choose compact/explanatory alternatives only when requested or justified by audience/venue.
