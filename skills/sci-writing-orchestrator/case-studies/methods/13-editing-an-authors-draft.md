# Case 13: editing an author's draft without losing their voice

A method study for polishing, deep revision and supervisor-requested changes to text someone else wrote. The goal is the author's paper at expert-journal standard, not the editor's paper.

## Read before touching anything
1. Read the whole draft once without editing. Note the main claim, the order of argument, and the author's habits: how they name actors, their tense pattern, spelling variant, citation style, preferred terms, and how long their sentences run.
2. Read the cited sources for the paragraphs that carry numbers. Most serious problems in a draft are factual (a wrong qualifier, a value from another condition, a claim the source does not make), and no amount of polish fixes them.
3. Then go section by section, and finish with a pass over the whole text for consistency.

## What to keep
- **Terms.** Keep the author's term for each concept when it matches the cited literature. Change it only when it conflicts with the field's usage or with another term in the same draft, and record the change in the terminology table.
- **Voice markers.** Keep "we" where the author uses it for their own analysis, their way of introducing studies, and their characteristic sentence openings, as long as they do not repeat on every sentence.
- **Structure that works.** Keep the author's section order and paragraph breaks unless a paragraph answers two questions or two paragraphs answer one.
- **Sentences that read well.** A sentence that is correct, clear and within the length profile keeps its wording. Editing it only to "improve" it adds risk and erases the voice.

## What to change
| Problem | Change |
|---|---|
| nominalisations ("achieves a reduction of") | the verb ("reduces") |
| a vague verb for evidence ("shows", "indicates" for a simulation) | the verb that says what was done: measured, simulated, fitted, estimated, reported |
| a quantity named loosely ("performance", "efficiency") | the quantity's own name and unit, as the source defines it |
| synonyms rotated for variety ("device", "element", "unit" for one object) | one term throughout |
| a qualifier lost or added ("up to" becoming plain, "about" dropped) | the source's qualifier |
| hype and stock phrases | the measured fact with its condition |
| defensive hedges | the fact with its scope and the next step |
| a paragraph written as an inventory or a template | a rewrite from the facts list by the line-of-thought guide |

## Precise wording across fields
| Field | Imprecise | Precise |
|---|---|---|
| devices | "high speed" | the switching time with its drive condition |
| chemistry | "good yield" | isolated yield, scale and purification |
| biology | "significantly increased" | the fold change, the test, n and the P value |
| clinical | "effective" | the effect size on the primary endpoint with its confidence interval |
| machine learning | "state-of-the-art accuracy" | the metric on the named benchmark and split, against the named baseline |
| ecology | "abundant" | density per unit area with the sampling method and season |
| economics | "large effect" | the elasticity or percentage change with the identification strategy |

## Handling comments from supervisors and co-authors
- Turn each comment into a decision: adopt, adopt in part, or decline with a reason. Adopt what narrows the scope or matches the source.
- When a comment asks for a stronger claim than the evidence supports, write the strongest claim the evidence does support and tell the author why.
- When the author rewrites a section themselves, keep their structure and check it against the sources: tightening often drops a qualifier, adds a purpose the source does not state, or shifts an order of magnitude.

## Report the edit
List what changed and why in a short table per section, the factual corrections with their source pages, the terms unified, and the open questions for the author. Mark the sentences that need the author's judgement rather than changing them silently.
