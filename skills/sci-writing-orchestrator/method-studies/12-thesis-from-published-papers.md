# Case 12: assembling a thesis from published and draft papers

A method study for doctoral and master's theses that collect several papers into one argued document. University rules on prior publication, co-authorship statements and format come first.

## The problem
Papers written for different journals repeat each other's background, use different terms and symbols for the same thing, and each ends as if it were the last word. Stapled together, they read as a collection.

## Method
1. **Write the research questions first.** Two to four questions that the whole thesis answers. Each later chapter answers one of them, or one part of one.
2. **Map papers to questions.** A table with one row per chapter: question, paper (or draft), main result with its condition, what the chapter leaves open, and which chapter takes that up. A chapter that leaves nothing open for the next ends the argument and belongs last.
3. **Build one terminology table and one symbol list** from all the papers before editing any chapter. When papers disagree, choose one term and record the alternatives in the table (sci-prose-style, terminology-template.md).
4. **Write the literature review around the questions.** Chapter 2 is organised by what each question needs to know, not by method or by year. It ends with the specific gaps that Chapters 3 to N address.
5. **Edit each paper into a chapter.**
   - Replace its introduction with one linking paragraph: the question this chapter answers, what the previous chapter established, and why that leads here.
   - Cut background that Chapter 2 already gives; point to it instead.
   - Unify terms and symbols with the table; renumber figures and equations by chapter (Fig. 3.4, Eq. 3.2).
   - Replace the paper's conclusion with a chapter summary that states what was shown and what the next chapter takes up.
6. **Write the general discussion last.** Answer each research question in turn with the evidence from the chapters, then state the limits that cross chapters and the next experiments, with measurable targets.
7. **Write Chapter 1 from the finished thesis.** Problem, gap, research questions, contributions per chapter (one paragraph each), and the thesis structure.

## The chain between chapters
Each chapter opening answers the private question "what did the previous chapter leave unresolved?" without voicing it. Constructed shape: "Chapter ⟨n−1⟩ showed that ⟨result⟩ under ⟨condition⟩, but the ⟨quantity⟩ that limits it was not measured. This chapter measures ⟨quantity⟩ in ⟨system⟩ and relates it to ⟨result⟩."

## Field variations
| Field | What changes |
|---|---|
| experimental sciences | methods may be pooled in one chapter if they are shared; chapter-specific methods stay in the chapter |
| computational | a shared software chapter or appendix with versions and seeds avoids repetition |
| clinical and public health | ethics approvals and registrations per study; a pooled limitations section is common |
| social sciences | a theory chapter may sit between the review and the empirical chapters |
| monograph-style theses | the same chain applies, but chapters are not papers; the linking paragraphs carry more of the argument |

## Checks
- Each chapter opens on the question the previous one left and closes on what the next one takes up.
- No background paragraph appears twice.
- One term and one symbol per concept throughout; the abstract, Chapter 1 and the general discussion state the same main results with the same numbers.
- Co-authorship statements and permissions for reused published material are complete.
