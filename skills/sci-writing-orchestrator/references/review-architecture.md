# Review architecture across disciplines

Choose an organizing axis that answers the review question: mechanisms, themes, competing theories, methods, historical development, populations, applications or a combination. Make the selection basis clear and compare evidence within consistent criteria. No domain hierarchy is imposed.

Give the review's contribution relative to previous work, its coverage limits, synthesis and unanswered questions. Comparator coverage has no fixed number of sentences; provide enough detail for the actual comparison. The default precise profile's sentence-length targets still apply. Do not exclude a scientifically important direction merely because it has fewer papers.

For systematic/scoping reviews select the appropriate protocol/search/appraisal/synthesis branch. Narrative reviews still need honest selection scope, source checks and balanced interpretation. Use the cross-domain case bank to select relevant public reporting/source references.

## Narrative review voice
Organize the argument around the question, mechanism, method or competing interpretation. For an important study, connect the problem, idea, realization and result, then explain how it changed the field. Use named authors and a verified year when the work's identity matters; otherwise make the scientific concept the grammatical subject. Apply one attribution convention consistently.

Separate synthesis from a sequence of paper summaries. State where studies agree, why apparently conflicting results differ and which conditions determine the comparison. Do not turn internal library counts, file states or evidence-audit tags into the review's contribution. Keep measured/simulated distinctions and access limitations visible where they affect a claim.

Systematic and scoping reviews are a distinct branch: report searches, screening counts, appraisal and selection decisions as required by their protocol. Do not remove legitimate methodological reporting to satisfy a narrative-review preference.

## Paragraph endings and comparisons
Open each subsection with its scientific question. Develop a linked account of the evidence, then end with the synthesis, design implication or next discriminating question. A generic promise about future work does not replace that conclusion. Comparison tables retain conditions, accounting scopes and reference metadata. Refer to a table where the argument needs it; avoid repeating every row or imposing a one-citation quota.

State gaps at the level the evidence supports. Preserve negative findings and necessary uncertainty. Avoid claiming that a field has never addressed a question unless a documented search supports that absence claim. A proposed experiment stays proposed.
