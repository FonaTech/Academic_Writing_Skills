# Proposals and theses

Match the current funding/university template and the actual discipline. Proposals distinguish prior evidence, preliminary results and planned research. Each aim has a clear question, applicable method, success/decision criteria where appropriate, risks, alternatives and resources. Do not impose a universal number of aims, a required numerical target or a single agency's headings. Verify the scheme's current instructions before using it.

Theses develop connected research questions and a coherent body of evidence/reasoning. Empirical, theoretical, qualitative and practice-based chapters can require different structures. Maintain terminology, symbols, cross-references, attribution and publication/coauthorship information across chapters. Follow institutional requirements for source reuse and final format.

Default precise style remains active. A proposal's expected outcome is written as planned/conditional, not as an observed result. Author acceptance and actual research obligations remain separate from prose and construction checks.
