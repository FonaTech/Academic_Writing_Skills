# Writing plan

For a large new manuscript or structural revision, keep a short living plan: question/type/audience; source ownership and constraints; confirmed decisions and assumptions; proposed section questions and argument; supporting and contrary evidence; raw-data/analysis needs; figure plan; languages/output formats; milestones and review scope; open items and definition of done.

Revise it when evidence or author decisions change. Do not create a large planning artifact for a bounded edit. A plan records authorized work and does not imply publication, third-party messaging or new experiments were approved.

## Reusable plan outline for substantial work
Keep the current plan in workflow/writing_plan.md when the project has no established location. Record its state as draft/current/stale/superseded and include:

1. Context, request, authoritative sources, collaborators and archived version.
2. Input drafts, data, references, reviewer/meeting decisions and their dates.
3. Audit findings: attribution, quantities/scopes, argument, terminology, figures and build issues.
4. Confirmed choices and reversible assumptions: structure, venue, languages, figures and completion scope.
5. Section tree with each section's question, argument, supporting/contrary evidence and word budget if requested.
6. Figures/tables: purpose, panel types, sources, print size and placement.
7. Content/build architecture and any requested length/language variants.
8. Milestones: evidence, incremental draft, displays, translation if requested, build, review, repair and delivery.
9. Checks and actual reviewer roles, their coverage and limitations.
10. Open decisions and missing materials that materially affect the conclusion.

Link long audit/reference lists separately. Update affected parts after a structural change and record why a dated decision was superseded. Existing authorization remains valid; planning does not create another approval requirement.
